§5

The night-light debugging saga

The lights were completely invisible through five different fixes, each confirmed wrong by direct pixel sampling (not guessed away) before moving to the next hypothesis:

wrong · attempt 1

Light quads placed just inside the solid mesh surface (offset sign error) — the opaque facade depth-occluded its own lights.

wrong · attempt 2

Fixed the offset direction, but candidates were bucketed into per-floor Y bands using the mesh's own sparse (10,740-tri) vertex density — most bands had too few source vertices and collapsed to duplicate positions.

wrong · attempt 3

Switched to ray-mesh intersection for true (taper-aware) surface points — positions became correct, still invisible. Root-caused via InstancedMesh vs. a plain Mesh at the identical transform: the plain mesh rendered fine, InstancedMesh did not, in this exact three.js r128 build.

wrong · attempt 4

Replaced InstancedMesh with one manually-merged BufferGeometry (still 1 draw call) — still invisible. Isolating by hiding every other scene object proved the lights did render (2,583 bright pixels appeared) — meaning something else in the full scene was painting over or occluding them specifically.

root cause, confirmed

Hiding just the building's own facade/glass mesh (nothing else) made the lights appear in the full scene. The face-normal-based "push outward" pointed away from the camera at corner-adjacent candidates (confirmed via dot product with the to-camera vector: negative), pushing lights further from the viewer instead of clear of the surface. Fixed by pushing toward the camera instead, which is correct by construction.

Performance tradeoff, disclosed: a true per-candidate occlusion re-check (raycast from camera to each candidate, confirming nothing closer) was implemented and worked, but against this mesh's raw vertex count with no spatial index, a dense-enough grid to reliably land 8-14 surviving lights took 6-9 seconds of blocking computation. Dropped in favor of the fixed camera-push heuristic alone (confirmed correct, just not verified per-candidate), which is fast (2.5-5s including the GLB load) but occasionally lands outside the 8-14 target (observed range: 4-13 across repeated rebuilds at the same settings). If this ships, precomputing a fixed set of known-good local positions once (rather than raycasting at runtime) removes this cost entirely.
