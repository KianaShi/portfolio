Phase 2§4

Placement — what didn't work first

Brief offered two placement options: bottom-left, or far-right edge. Bottom-left was rejected outright — the Space Needle occupies that whole side of the window at ground level with nothing to hide behind. Far-right was chosen, but the first two attempts placed it in the open gap between Skyscraper07 and the PC tower (no occluder there), and without something to hide its lower body behind, the whole tall mesh rendered top-to-bottom and swallowed Rainier's peak:

(This is the final, correct placement, shown here for the silhouette — see §5 for the before/after.) Re-solved to sit almost entirely BEHIND the PC tower instead (PC's z is 0.09-1.0, much nearer camera than this building's z=-0.85) — the same occlusion trick Skyscraper07 uses with the monitor. Only the swept roof cap and a few window rows above the PC's own screen-top (~258px) end up visible, and the whole footprint sits at screen x≥1015, clearing Rainier's ~830-1010px band entirely.

Phase 2§9

Does the second building actually help?

Yes, modestly

Comparing the one-building and two-building shots side by side (§5): the second silhouette (different height, different x, nearer z, genuinely different architecture — a swept roof cap vs. Skyscraper07's flat setback) reads as a real second depth plane, not a duplicate. The stronger depth cue this round is actually the background blur (§1-2) — it affects the entire backdrop at once, while the second building only adds depth information in one corner of the frame. Together they're complementary: blur separates "far" from "near," the second building adds a middle step between "near" and "nearer." Given the placement constraint (had to hide behind the PC tower to avoid Rainier and stay off the Space Needle), there isn't much room left for a third building without it either crowding Residential_04 or needing yet another occluder to invent — matches the brief's own instinct to cap at 2 by default.
