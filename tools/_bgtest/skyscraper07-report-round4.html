Follow-up to Round 3's one open item: the window-light layer's runtime raycast approach produced an unstable 4-13 visible count and, on inspection this round, some raycast-approved positions were quietly failing the real GPU depth test. Replaced with a hand-verified, hardcoded position list. All other Round 3 parameters (scale, position, materials) are unchanged and not re-tested here.

§1

Verdict

Ready

The light-count instability is fixed: default night state now shows exactly 9 window lights, every run, confirmed by reading back actual rendered pixel colors (not just projection math). All acceptance items from Round 3's checklist now pass, including the one that was previously a warning. One caveat worth flagging: the 9 lights concentrate on a single strongly-visible facade plus one corner accent rather than splitting evenly across two faces — see §3.

§2

What was actually wrong

Round 3's light placement used a raycast against the building mesh, pushed 0.22 world units toward the camera, and trusted the raycaster's own front-to-back hit order to decide whether a candidate cleared the facade. That assumption was wrong on this decimated (10,740-tri) mesh:

attempt 1 · round 3's own approach, re-examined

The raycaster said a light was nearer than the facade for all 22 original candidates. In the real GPU render, only 5 of 9 hardcoded picks actually showed the lit color at their predicted pixel — a neighboring parapet/setback triangle, not the one the ray hit, was sitting closer to the camera at that same screen position and winning the depth test anyway.

attempt 2 · denser grid, batch pixel-readback

Re-derived from a much denser 220×90 exploration grid (129 deduplicated candidates), verified by rendering all of them at once as one all-white test mesh and sampling each one's predicted pixel. 80/129 read back lit. This also turned out unreliable: testing 129 overlapping quads simultaneously let an unoccluded neighbor's quad "cover for" a genuinely-occluded candidate at readback time. 4 of the 11 positions chosen this way turned out to be false positives once rendered alone.

attempt 3 · root-caused, fixed

Every candidate re-tested one at a time — a single quad added to the scene alone, rendered, sampled, removed, next candidate — the only way to rule out neighbor contamination. Of ~5 distinct facade-normal directions in the visible band, only one face (normal ≈ (0.198, 0, −0.980)) and one corner normal read reliably lit in isolation (25/25 and 1/1). The near-Z face and the +X face, which looked fine in attempts 1–2, mostly failed individually too (2/11 and 0/8) — confirming the depth-occlusion theory, not a readback bug. The reliable pair was enough on its own to hit the 8-14 target: 9 positions, greedily thinned to a minimum 14px on-screen separation so they read as distinct windows instead of merging into blobs (which is what the first hardcoded attempt did — see the before/after below).

Also fixed in the same pass: the light quad size (0.045/0.021 of facade bbox) was tuned for the old runtime-computed spread, and at the new hardcoded positions' tighter natural spacing it made adjacent lights overlap into solid blobs. Shrunk to 0.020/0.011 — narrower, more window-shaped, and small enough to read as separate panes at this spacing.

§7

Recommendation

Ready to move to production evaluation, with one honest caveat

Final parameters (already the defaults in tools/_bgtest/building-test.js): variant optimized, scale B (0.035), position X4 (x=0.40), rotation 15°, material P2 (Δ10%), max light intensity 0.50, 9 hardcoded window lights.

  1. The light layer is now a fixed array (HARDCODED_LIGHTS in building-test.js) tied to this exact composition. If scale or X preset changes later, re-derive positions with window.__buildingTest.regenerateFromRaycast() and re-verify each candidate individually before hardcoding — batch verification is not reliable on this mesh (see §2).
  2. The 9-lights-on-one-facade result is a real constraint of this decimated mesh's geometry at this camera angle, not a placement bug. If a more even 2-facade split is wanted later, it would need either a less-decimated mesh (better normals near the setback/parapet edges) or a different push strategy that accounts for neighboring-triangle occlusion, not just the immediate hit triangle.
  3. All other Round 3 findings (scale/position/material choices) stand unchanged — this round only touched the light layer.
